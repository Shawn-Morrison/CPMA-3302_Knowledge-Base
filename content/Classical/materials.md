# Materials Used in Classical Guitars

[[Classical/index|Classical]] guitars are built from a combination of carefully selected woods that affect look, tone, weight, and durability. Musicians often choose a [[Classical/index|Classical]] guitar because specific [[Classical/materials|materials]] produce the warm and balanced sound required for many [[Classical/style|styles]] of music.

## Common Tonewoods

Different woods shape the sound of classical guitars in noticeable ways.

- Cedar – warm and responsive tone
- Spruce – bright and articulate sound
- Rosewood – rich overtones and sustain
- Mahogany – balanced and focused tone

### Traditional Construction Materials

Classical guitars commonly feature:

- Solid cedar or spruce tops
- Rosewood or mahogany backs and sides
- Ebony or rosewood fretboards

## Choose Wisely

> The choice of materials significantly affects projection, sustain, and tonal warmth. Many classical musicians carefully select woods based on the specific sound characteristics they prefer. Material choices can also affect [[Classical/construction|construction]], [[Classical/maintenance|maintenance]], and [[Classical/price|price]].

#### Common Top Wood Comparison

- Cedar – warmer and quicker response
- Spruce – brighter and develops over time
